WELCOME — A NOTE TO INTERNATIONAL PLAYERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You have been invited to play FROM THE FIELD TO THE FORTUNE on behalf of your
company's leadership team. As an executive of a Fortune 100 firm exploring
opportunities in the billion-dollar industry of American college football, you
may be wondering: why does college football matter so much in the United States,
and specifically in the American South?

This game was designed to answer exactly that question.

Unlike professional sports, college football in America is not just a business —
it is a cultural institution. It has shaped the identity of entire regions,
reflected the racial and social struggles of a nation, survived two World Wars,
and grown from humble 1890s scrimmages into a multi-billion dollar media empire.
Understanding its history is essential to understanding the opportunity your
firm is evaluating.

As you play, you will not just be buying programs and collecting revenue. You
will travel through more than 130 years of American history — from the first
Southern football games in the 1890s, through the painful but transformative era
of racial integration, through World War II, the Civil Rights Movement, and into
the modern controversies of NIL deals, the transfer portal, and conference
realignment. Every card you draw, every program you acquire, and every event
you encounter is grounded in real history.

By the time the game ends, you will understand not only why college football is
worth billions of dollars — but why it matters to the people who love it.

Welcome to the game. Welcome to the South.


================================================================================
SECTION 1: THE GAME IN BRIEF
================================================================================

FROM THE FIELD TO THE FORTUNE is a property-acquisition game about building a
college football empire. Players begin as program commissioners in the 1890s
with modest resources and work to acquire, develop, and profit from college
football programs across eight historical eras of American history.

Players buy programs, collect program revenue from opponents, build Recruiting
Classes and Stadium Expansions to increase their earnings, and navigate the
turbulent events of American history — from World War II drafts to the Civil
Rights Movement to the modern NIL era.

The wealthiest commissioner standing at the end of the game wins.


================================================================================
SECTION 2: THE SEVEN THEMES OF COLLEGE FOOTBALL HISTORY
================================================================================

This game is built around seven essential themes. Each is woven into the board,
the cards, and the program groups you will encounter during play. Read these
before you begin — they will give you the context you need to understand what
is happening as you play.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 1: The Growth of College Football in the South
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
College football in the American South began in the early 1890s, decades after
the game first appeared in the Northeast. Southern universities like Auburn,
Vanderbilt, and Georgia quickly adopted the sport, and it soon took on a
significance that went far beyond athletics. In a region still recovering from
the Civil War and searching for identity, football became a source of pride,
community, and regional rivalry. By the mid-20th century, the South was the
undisputed heartland of college football culture.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 2: Eight Reasons College Football Is Different in the South
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. IDENTITY: In Southern states, the university team IS the regional identity.
   People who never attended a school will still bleed its colors for life.

2. NO PRO COMPETITION: For decades, the South had no NFL franchises. The
   college game was the only game in town — and it filled that void completely.

3. SMALL TOWN ECONOMIES: Schools like Auburn, Alabama, and Clemson are located
   in small towns. On game day, the stadium is one of the largest gatherings of
   people in the entire state.

4. RELIGIOUS DEVOTION: The culture around Southern football resembles religious
   practice — Saturday rituals, sacred stadiums, legendary coaches treated like
   prophets, and a devotion passed down through generations.

5. COACHING CULTURE: Legendary coaches like Bear Bryant (Alabama), Vince Dooley
   (Georgia), and Bobby Bowden (Florida State) became cultural icons, not just
   sports figures.

6. INTEGRATION AS A FLASHPOINT: The integration of Southern college football
   teams was among the most public and contested civil rights battles in the
   region's history. Football was the arena where racial change was most visible.

7. SEC DOMINANCE: The Southeastern Conference (SEC) has won more national
   championships than any other conference and is widely considered the most
   competitive — and most lucrative — conference in college football.

8. THE STADIUM EXPERIENCE: Southern stadiums like Bryant-Denny (Alabama),
   Tiger Stadium (LSU), and Death Valley (Clemson) are among the largest
   sporting venues in the world, with atmospheres unlike anything in
   professional sports.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 3: The Integration of College Football
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For decades, Southern college football was racially segregated. Black athletes
were barred from playing at major Southern universities. The integration of
college football was slow, painful, and contested. It accelerated after a
famous 1970 game in which USC's integrated team defeated all-white Alabama —
an event that reportedly convinced Bear Bryant to begin recruiting Black players.
By the mid-1970s, most Southern programs were integrated. The influx of Black
talent transformed the game and the culture around it permanently.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 4: The Billion-Dollar Industry
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
College football today generates over $8 billion annually. Television contracts
alone — most notably the SEC's deals with ESPN and CBS — are worth hundreds of
millions of dollars per year. The College Football Playoff (CFP) distributes
billions in revenue to conferences. Head coaches earn $8-10 million per year.
Stadiums seat 80,000–107,000 fans. This is not a small business. It is one of
the most lucrative sports enterprises in the world — and it is built entirely
on the labor of unpaid (until very recently) college students.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 5: The Racial Context of College Football
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Race and college football have been intertwined since the beginning. Before
integration, Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) developed their own
football tradition — one that produced brilliant players who were invisible to
the mainstream white programs. After integration, Black athletes became the
backbone of the sport's greatest teams, yet the coaching ranks and athletic
department leadership remained overwhelmingly white. Today, the majority of
players at top programs are Black, while most head coaches and administrators
are white — a disparity that continues to generate debate.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 6: Broader Social Events That Shaped the Game
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
College football did not exist in a vacuum. The Great Depression forced programs
to cut budgets and play shortened seasons. World War II canceled entire seasons
and sent thousands of players to combat, fundamentally changing rosters and
rivalries. The Civil Rights Movement forced universities to confront segregation
in the most public way possible — through the football field. The Cold War,
Vietnam protests on campuses, and the social upheaval of the 1960s all left
their marks on the game.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEME 7: Current Issues Facing College Football
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
College football is in the middle of its most disruptive era since integration:

- NIL (Name, Image & Likeness): Since 2021, college athletes can earn money
  from endorsements, appearances, and social media. A star quarterback might
  earn millions before ever playing a professional game.

- The Transfer Portal: Players can now transfer to any school without sitting
  out a year. This has created a free-agent marketplace and fundamentally
  changed how rosters are built each season.

- Revenue Share: As of 2025, schools are beginning to share direct revenue
  with athletes — a seismic shift from the "amateur" model that defined college
  sports for over a century.

- Conference Realignment: Traditional geographic conferences have been
  shattered by billion-dollar TV deals. USC and UCLA left the Pac-12 for the
  Big Ten. Texas and Oklahoma left for the SEC. The map of college football is
  being redrawn by money, not tradition.

SECTION 5: SETUP
================================================================================

1. Each player selects a team helmet token and places it on KICKOFF (GO).

2. The Commissioner (Banker) gives each player $15 MILLION in Gridiron Dollars,
   distributed as follows:
       Two x $5M
       Four x $1M
       One x $500K
       One x $200K
       Five x $100K

3. One player is elected Commissioner (Banker). If there are more than four
   players, the Commissioner may choose to manage the bank only and not play.
   The Commissioner holds all unowned Program Deeds, Recruiting Class tokens,
   and Stadium Expansion tokens until they are purchased by players.

4. Shuffle the Gridiron Events cards and Commissioner's Office cards separately.
   Place each deck face-down in their designated board spaces.

5. Players roll both dice. The player with the highest roll goes first.
   Play passes to the left.

   The Commissioner's bank never goes bankrupt. If Gridiron Dollars run low,
   IOUs written on paper are acceptable.

SECTION 6: THE BOARD — SPACE GUIDE
================================================================================

The board is divided into 40 spaces traveled clockwise. Below is a summary
of every space type you will encounter.

--- CORNER SPACES ---

KICKOFF (GO)
  Collect $2M in Gridiron Dollars each time you land on or pass this space.
  This represents the annual salary and stipend paid to commissioners.

NCAA PROBATION (Jail / Just Visiting)
  If you are sent here, you are under investigation. See SECTION 11 for rules.
  If you were not sent here, you are "Just Visiting" — no penalty applies.

NIL SIGNING DAY (Free Parking)
  You rest here until your next turn. No penalty. You may conduct business as
  usual (collect revenue, negotiate trades, etc.). Think of it as the quiet

SECTION 7: PLAYING THE GAME
================================================================================

On your turn, roll both dice and move your token clockwise around the board
the number of spaces shown. The space you land on determines what happens.

You may do one of the following, depending on where you land:

  - Acquire an unowned Program, Bowl Game, or TV Contract
  - Pay program revenue to the owner of the space you landed on
  - Pay taxes
  - Draw a Gridiron Events or Commissioner's Office card
  - Go to NCAA Probation
  - Rest on NIL Signing Day (Free Parking)
  - Collect $2M by passing or landing on Kickoff (GO)
  - Be "Just Visiting" at NCAA Probation

Two or more tokens may rest on the same space simultaneously.

--- DOUBLES ---
If you roll doubles, take your turn as normal, then roll again for a bonus turn.
If you roll doubles a third time in a row, go directly to NCAA Probation.

--- PASSING KICKOFF (GO) ---
Every time you pass or land on KICKOFF, collect $2M from the Commissioner.
You may collect $2M twice in one turn if, for example, a card sends you
forward past Kickoff. However, you do NOT collect $2M when sent to
NCAA Probation, or when sent backward by a card.


================================================================================
SECTION 8: ACQUIRING PROGRAMS, BOWL GAMES, AND TV CONTRACTS
================================================================================

When you land on an unowned Program space, you have the FIRST RIGHT to buy it
from the Commissioner at the printed price.

If you buy it, pay the Commissioner and receive that Program's Deed card, which
you keep face-up in front of you as proof of ownership.

If you decline to buy, the Commissioner immediately AUCTIONS the program to the
highest bidder, starting at any price another player is willing to pay. Even
if you declined the purchase, you may participate in the auction.

The same rules apply to Bowl Games and TV Contracts.


================================================================================
SECTION 9: BOWL GAMES (Replaces Railroads)
================================================================================

Bowl Games represent the postseason revenue opportunities that fuel college
football's billion-dollar economy. There are four Bowl Game spaces:

    Rose Bowl, Sugar Bowl, Orange Bowl, Cotton Bowl

If a Bowl Game is unowned when you land on it, you may buy it for $200M.

Revenue collected from opponents who land on a Bowl Game you own:
    Own 1 Bowl Game:  Collect $25M
    Own 2 Bowl Games: Collect $50M
    Own 3 Bowl Games: Collect $100M
    Own 4 Bowl Games: Collect $200M

Bowl Games may be mortgaged but not built upon.

Historical Note: Bowl games were originally corporate sponsorship events
designed to promote tourism and local economies. The Sugar Bowl (New Orleans),
Cotton Bowl (Dallas), Orange Bowl (Miami), and Rose Bowl (Pasadena) became
among the most prestigious events in college sports — and among the most
lucrative. Today, the College Football Playoff has largely replaced the bowl
system as the marquee postseason event.


================================================================================
SECTION 10: TV CONTRACTS (Replaces Utilities)
================================================================================

Television contracts are the engine of college football's financial empire.
There are two TV Contract spaces:

    ESPN Contract
    CBS / SEC Network Contract

If unowned when you land on it, you may buy it for $150M.

Revenue from opponents who land on a TV Contract space you own is determined
by the dice roll that sent them there:
    If you own 1 TV Contract:  Opponent pays 4x their dice roll (in $M)
    If you own both TV Contracts: Opponent pays 10x their dice roll (in $M)

Historical Note: ESPN's $7.3 billion deal with the SEC (signed 2023, running
through 2034) is the most valuable media rights deal in college sports history.
Before this era, major college programs earned a fraction of what they earn
today. Television transformed college football from a regional pastime into
a national industry.


================================================================================
SECTION 11: COLLECTING PROGRAM REVENUE
================================================================================

When you land on a Program space owned by another player, you must pay that
player the revenue shown on their Deed card.

The owner MUST ask for revenue before the next player rolls the dice. If the
owner fails to ask before then, they forfeit the revenue for that visit.

--- CONFERENCE MONOPOLY (Color Group Complete) ---
If a player owns ALL programs within an era group (color group), the revenue
on any unbuilt program in that group is DOUBLED. This represents the
compounding advantage of owning a complete conference.

Exception: Double revenue is NOT collected if any program in the group
is currently mortgaged.

--- REVENUE SCALE (on each Program Deed) ---
Revenue increases as you build Recruiting Classes and Stadium Expansions.
See SECTION 12 for full building rules and revenue tables.

Revenue is NOT collected on mortgaged programs.


================================================================================
SECTION 12: RECRUITING CLASSES (Replaces Houses)
================================================================================

Once you own all programs in an era group (Conference Monopoly), you may
begin adding RECRUITING CLASSES to your programs. Recruiting Classes
represent the investment in talent that drives program revenue — the better
your class, the more valuable your program becomes.

BUILDING RULES:
- You may build Recruiting Classes on your turn or between other players' turns.
- You must build EVENLY: you cannot build a second Recruiting Class on any
  one program until every program in the group has at least one.
- Maximum of 4 Recruiting Classes per program (before a Stadium Expansion).
- You may NOT build if any program in the color group is mortgaged.
- Costs are shown on each Program Deed card.

SELLING RECRUITING CLASSES:
- Sell back to the Commissioner at HALF the purchase price.
- Must also be sold evenly (sell one from each program in the group before
  selling a second from any one program).

Historical Note: The annual recruiting cycle is one of the most intense and
scrutinized events in college football. National Signing Day — when high school
players officially commit to programs — is treated as a major media event.
The emergence of the transfer portal has added a second annual "recruiting"
cycle for experienced players.


================================================================================
SECTION 13: STADIUM EXPANSIONS (Replaces Hotels)
================================================================================

A STADIUM EXPANSION is the highest level of program development. It replaces
4 Recruiting Classes and dramatically increases the revenue your program
generates.

BUILDING RULES:
- You must have 4 Recruiting Classes on EACH program in the color group before
  building a Stadium Expansion on any one of them.
- When you build a Stadium Expansion, return the 4 Recruiting Classes to the
  Commissioner and pay the additional cost shown on the Deed.
- Only ONE Stadium Expansion may be built per program.

Historical Note: The stadiums of the American South are among the largest
in the world. Bryant-Denny Stadium (Alabama) seats 101,821. Tiger Stadium (LSU)
seats 102,321. Neyland Stadium (Tennessee) seats 101,915. These are not just
sports venues — they are economic engines for their surrounding communities,
generating hundreds of millions of dollars per season in local spending.

  8. "THE GREAT DEPRESSION — PAY $10K PER PROGRAM YOU OWN"
     The economic collapse of the 1930s hit college athletic budgets hard.
     Multiple programs nearly folded. Pay $10K for each program deed you hold.
     [Theme: Broader Social Events — Great Depression]

  9. "GO TO NCAA PROBATION"
     [Theme: NCAA violations]

  10. "COLLECT $25K FROM EVERY PLAYER — BOWL GAME PAYOUT"
      Your conference negotiated a strong bowl payout. Collect $25K from
      every other player.
      [Theme: Billion-Dollar Industry — bowl revenue]

  11. "DESEGREGATION ORDER — HISTORICAL MOMENT"
      Read aloud: "In 1963, University of Alabama Governor George Wallace
      stood in the schoolhouse door to block Black students from enrolling.
      Football integration followed years later — and changed the program
      forever." No financial penalty. This is a moment of education.
      [Theme: Integration / Racial Context / Civil Rights]

  12. "PAY SCHOOL TAXES — $100K OR 10% OF ASSETS, WHICHEVER IS LESS"
      [Theme: General gameplay — tax mechanic]

  13. "YOUR PROGRAM GOES ON A BOWL STREAK — COLLECT $300K"
      Six consecutive bowl appearances bring in TV revenue and recruiting
      momentum. Collect $300K from the Commissioner.
      [Theme: Billion-Dollar Industry]

  14. "ESCAPE NCAA INVESTIGATION"
      Keep this card. Use it to leave NCAA Probation free, or sell it.
      [Theme: Current Issues — NCAA compliance]

  15. "TRANSFER PORTAL BRINGS YOU A 5-STAR QUARTERBACK — COLLECT $200K"
      A star transfer elevates your program's profile and ticket sales.
      Collect $200K from the Commissioner.
      [Theme: Current Issues — Transfer Portal]

  16. "THE CIVIL RIGHTS ACT OF 1964 — INTEGRATE YOUR PROGRAM"
      If you own any program in the Pink (Depression/Wartime) or Orange
      (Integration Era) group, collect $250K as your program modernizes.
      If you own no programs in these groups, collect $50K.
      This card reflects how integration ultimately strengthened programs
      that embraced it — while those that resisted fell behind.
      [Theme: Integration / Civil Rights Movement]

SECTION 17: SPECIAL SPACES
================================================================================

--- NCAA PROBATION (Jail) ---

You are sent to NCAA Probation if:
  - You land on the GO TO COMMISSIONER'S OFFICE space
  - A Gridiron Events or Commissioner's Office card sends you there
  - You roll doubles three times in a row

When sent to Probation, your turn ends immediately. You do NOT collect $2M
even if you passed Kickoff on the way there.

While under Probation, you CAN still collect program revenue from opponents
who land on your properties.

TO GET OUT OF PROBATION, you may:
  - Pay a fine of $50K and move on your next turn
  - Use an "Escape NCAA Investigation" card (if you have one)
  - Purchase an "Escape NCAA Investigation" card from another player
    at a mutually agreed price
  - Wait up to three turns, rolling the dice each turn trying to roll
    doubles. If you roll doubles, move out using that roll.
  - After three failed turns, you MUST pay $50K and move on the next turn.

Historical Note: NCAA Probation is a real and serious penalty. Programs
placed on probation may face scholarship reductions, bowl bans, recruiting
restrictions, and public humiliation. Southern Methodist University (SMU)
received the "death penalty" in 1987 — a complete suspension of their
football program for violations so severe the NCAA effectively shut them down.

--- NIL SIGNING DAY (Free Parking) ---

You simply rest here until your next turn. No penalty or reward.
You may still collect revenue from opponents who land on your properties.

Historical Note: The NIL era began July 1, 2021, when the NCAA finally allowed
college athletes to profit from their Name, Image, and Likeness. Within months,
top quarterbacks were earning millions from endorsements. NIL collectives —
groups of boosters pooling money to pay athletes — emerged at every major
program. The old model of "amateurism" was effectively over.

--- PROGRAM EXPENSES (Income Tax) ---

Pay $200K or 10% of your total assets (programs, buildings, cash, and
unmortgaged program values), whichever is lower. You must DECIDE which
you are paying before you calculate.

--- STADIUM RENOVATION TAX (Luxury Tax) ---

Pay $100K to the Commissioner.

SECTION 18: TRADING AND SELLING
================================================================================

You may trade or sell any undeveloped Program, Bowl Game, or TV Contract to
another player at any privately agreed price. Negotiations are encouraged —
and historically accurate. Conference commissioners negotiate TV deals behind
closed doors constantly.

RESTRICTIONS:
  - You may NOT sell a program if any Recruiting Classes or Stadium Expansions
    are built on ANY program in that color group. You must first sell all
    buildings back to the Commissioner at half price.
  - Players may not loan or borrow money from each other. Money may only
    be borrowed by mortgaging a property to the Commissioner.

SELLING BUILDINGS:
  - Recruiting Classes and Stadium Expansions are sold to the Commissioner ONLY
    (not to other players) at HALF their original purchase price.
  - Buildings must be sold EVENLY across the group, just as they were built.


================================================================================
SECTION 19: MORTGAGING PROGRAMS
================================================================================

If you need to raise cash, you may mortgage any of your programs to the
Commissioner (Bank). To mortgage a program:

  1. First sell all buildings on that color group back to the Commissioner.
  2. Turn the Program Deed face-down.
  3. Collect the mortgage value from the Commissioner (printed on the back
     of each Deed — half the purchase price).

You retain ownership of mortgaged programs. No other player can claim them.
You cannot collect revenue on a mortgaged program, but you CAN collect
revenue on other programs within the same color group (unless those are
also mortgaged).

TO LIFT A MORTGAGE:
Pay the Commissioner the mortgage value plus 10% interest.

SELLING MORTGAGED PROGRAMS:
You may sell mortgaged programs to other players at any agreed price.
The buyer must immediately pay 10% interest to the Commissioner, then may
either pay off the mortgage in full or retain it (paying another 10% when
eventually lifting it).


================================================================================
SECTION 20: BANKRUPTCY
================================================================================

If you owe more money than you can raise through all available means — cash,
selling buildings, and mortgaging properties — you are declared BANKRUPT
and eliminated from the game.

IF YOU GO BANKRUPT TO THE COMMISSIONER (BANK):
  The Commissioner takes all your cash and Program Deeds.
  All your programs are auctioned immediately to the remaining players.
  Return any "Escape NCAA Investigation" cards to the bottom of the deck.

IF YOU GO BANKRUPT TO ANOTHER PLAYER:
  That player receives all your cash, Program Deeds, and "Escape NCAA
  Investigation" cards. Your buildings are sold to the Commissioner at
  half price, and the proceeds go to the player who bankrupted you.
  If you have mortgaged programs, the receiving player must immediately
  pay 10% interest and then choose to lift the mortgage or retain it.

Historical Note: The threat of "bankruptcy" is not just a game mechanic —
it mirrors real economic pressures facing college athletic departments.
Over half of all Division I programs operate at a deficit, kept afloat
only by revenue from football and basketball. Schools in smaller conferences
earn fractions of what SEC schools earn, creating an ever-widening gap
in competitive resources.


================================================================================
SECTION 21: WINNING THE GAME
================================================================================

The last Commissioner standing — the only player who has not gone bankrupt —
is declared the winner.

OPTIONAL TIME LIMIT RULE (recommended for classroom play):
Before starting, agree on a time limit (suggested: 45-60 minutes). When time
expires, all players add up:
  1. Cash in hand
  2. Current value of all owned programs (at printed board price)
  3. Mortgaged programs at half their printed board price
  4. Recruiting Classes at their purchase price
  5. Stadium Expansions at purchase price (including Recruiting Classes traded in)

The player with the highest total is the winner.


================================================================================
SECTION 22: SHORT GAME RULES
================================================================================

For a faster game (recommended: 30-40 minutes):

1. SETUP: The Commissioner shuffles all Program Deed cards and deals 2 to each
   player. Each player immediately pays the Commissioner the printed price for
   each program they receive. Play begins as in the standard game.

2. BUILDING: Only 3 Recruiting Classes (instead of 4) are required on each
   program before building a Stadium Expansion. Stadium Expansion revenue
   remains the same.

3. END OF GAME: When the FIRST player goes bankrupt, the game ends immediately.
   All remaining players calculate their total wealth (cash + program values +
   buildings). The richest player wins.

SECTION 23: EDUCATIONAL DISCUSSION GUIDE
================================================================================

This game is designed not only for entertainment but for education. After
playing, consider discussing the following questions with your fellow players:

1. Why did college football take root so deeply in the American South, more
   than in any other region of the country?

2. What does it mean that racial integration of Southern football programs came
   so late — and so publicly? What does that tell us about the relationship
   between sports and social change?

3. The game models college football as a "billion-dollar industry." But for most
   of its history, the athletes generating that revenue were unpaid. How do
   you think about the ethics of that arrangement? How does the NIL era
   change it?

4. Conference realignment has shattered geographic traditions in favor of
   television dollars. Is this progress, or does it destroy something valuable?

5. If you were advising a Fortune 100 firm on how to enter the college football
   industry, what piece of this game's history would you most want them to
   understand before they invested?

SECTION 24: GLOSSARY FOR INTERNATIONAL PLAYERS
================================================================================

ATHLETIC DEPARTMENT: The university division that oversees all sports programs.
  At major schools, football revenue subsidizes every other sport.

BOWL GAME: A postseason game held at a neutral site, originally tied to tourist
  events in warm-weather cities. There are now over 40 bowl games annually.

CFP (College Football Playoff): The current postseason championship format,
  expanded to 12 teams in 2024. Distributes over $500M to conferences.

CONFERENCE: A group of universities that compete against each other regularly
  and share revenue from media contracts. The SEC is the most powerful.

HBCU (Historically Black College or University): Universities founded to serve
  Black students during and after segregation. HBCUs maintained their own
  football tradition, producing legendary coaches and players.

HEAD COACH: The lead coach of a football program. Top SEC coaches earn $8-10M
  per year — often more than any other employee at the university.

INTEGRATION: The process by which Black athletes were admitted to and allowed
  to play for previously all-white Southern programs. This process was
  slow, contested, and not complete until the early 1970s.

NIL (Name, Image, and Likeness): The right of college athletes (since 2021)
  to earn money from endorsements, appearances, and social media.

RECRUITING: The process of identifying, pursuing, and signing high school
  athletes to join a college program. Violations of recruiting rules are among
  the most common NCAA infractions.

REVENUE SHARE: The emerging model (2025) in which universities directly pay
  athletes a portion of program revenue — a dramatic departure from the
  traditional "amateur" model.

SEC (Southeastern Conference): The most dominant and most valuable conference
  in college football. Members include Alabama, Georgia, LSU, Tennessee,
  Florida, and others. Annual revenue exceeds $800 million.

TRANSFER PORTAL: The NCAA database through which players announce their intent
  to transfer to a new school. Since 2021, players can transfer freely and
  play immediately. Over 1,500 players enter the portal each year.
